Use cases

Who reads a feed for a living

AskNoema does one thing: it verifies media while the media is still playing. What that is worth depends on why you were watching in the first place. These are the four readings the product is built around, and the session all four of them share.

Who it is for

Four ways to need the same answer

Every one of these jobs already involves watching something and deciding whether to believe it. The work is the deciding, and until now it has happened in other tabs, from memory, or not at all.

Research · policy

Claims that move faster than the literature

A position circulates for weeks before anything peer-reviewed catches up with it. AskNoema hands you the claim in structured form the first time you meet it — extracted, checked against primary data and the live web, and kept with its lineage, so months later it can be cited or dismissed rather than half-remembered.

Newsrooms

A first pass that survives an editor

The question on a breaking clip is always the same three: what does it actually assert, what has it left out, and who said it first. One pass answers all three — transcript, on-screen text, extracted claims, and the earliest source found for each — so reporting starts from a record instead of a rewatch.

Trust · safety · integrity

Manipulation described, not merely flagged

A verdict is not a case. Every stage keeps its output inspectable — what was heard, what was seen on screen, which claim failed and against what — which is the whole difference between a score you have to take on faith and a finding you can escalate.

High-noise fields

Reasoning with the stream, not after it

Analysts, clinicians, educators, anyone whose field is discussed publicly faster than it is published. Session memory carries earlier analysis forward, so a week of watching compounds into something you can consult, instead of a feed you have already forgotten.

The session

Four things you do with it

Different audiences, one set of moves. These run inside the stream — nothing here asks you to leave the clip, open a tab, or file anything.

  1. 01

    Clip inspection

    Point at one video. Back comes what it claims, the evidence each claim rests on, the places those claims contradict each other, and the questions the clip never answers.

  2. 02

    Narrative comparison

    Put several clips on the same event beside each other. The overlay holds them against one another: where the accounts agree, where they diverge, and which side of a divergence is actually sourced.

  3. 03

    Session memory

    Nothing you have already settled gets asked twice. Verdicts, sources and open threads stay with the session, so the tenth clip on a topic starts where the ninth left off.

  4. 04

    Reasoning summaries

    A session ends as a short, inspectable account: what was checked, what held, what did not, and the trail back to every source that decided it.

Worked example

One clip, end to end

The same nine-stage pass runs whichever of the four you are doing. The product page lets you step through it; here is what it produces.

A forty-second clip, no caption, three assertions delivered as settled fact.

What arrives
Video, audio, whatever is burned into the frame, and the account that posted it. No transcript, no description, no citations.
What runs
Transcription, vision annotation and OCR read the clip; Episteme extracts the three claims; web verification puts each against primary sources; synthesis reconciles the results.
What comes back
A verdict per claim with a confidence score, the sources behind it in order of authority, and the reasoning trace that got there — on the clip, while it is still playing.

The nine stages are laid out one by one on the product page.

Boundaries

What it is not

A verification tool that overstates its own reach is the problem it claims to solve. Three things AskNoema is not built to be.

Not an arbiter

It checks claims, it does not settle disputes

Where the evidence is genuinely contested, the output says so and shows both sides of it. A confident verdict on an open question would be a failure of the system, not a feature of it.

Not a moderator

It reports to you, not about you

Findings go to the person who asked for them. AskNoema does not rank, remove, or report the content it reads, and it is not a pipeline into anyone else's enforcement decisions.

Not a replacement

It does the first pass, you do the judgement

The trace is inspectable precisely because you are expected to inspect it. The research this lab publishes is largely about what happens when people stop doing that.
